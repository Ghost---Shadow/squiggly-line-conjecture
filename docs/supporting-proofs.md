# Supporting proofs

This page lists everything in the repository that is proved beyond the
theorem itself. It has two parts: the results topic by topic, and the same
results paper by paper. The theorem and its proofs are in the
[README](../README.md#7-the-proofs). What is not proved yet is in
[`wishlist.md`](wishlist.md).

## By topic

Nothing in this section is needed for the theorem. It is the same handful of
tools (slope, nearness, travel and the kernel) pointed at puzzles that are
already known in deep learning. Each row is a statement about a simplified
model, most often one that is linear in its weights, with a plain-language
note and a Lean proof. Each note also says what it does **not** show. None of
them proves the phenomenon for real networks.

**How training moves.** Proofs in
[`lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training), notes in
[`docs/supporting/training/`](supporting/training).

| Topic | What is shown | Note | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Least action | Gradient descent moves only along the features, so any fit it reaches is the nearest one. | [note](supporting/training/least-action.md) | [`LeastAction`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/LeastAction.lean) |
| Spectral bias | The error along a direction of strength `λ` shrinks by `1 − ηλ` per step. Strong directions are learned first. | [note](supporting/training/spectral-bias.md) | [`SpectralBias`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/SpectralBias.lean) |
| Edge of stability | That error shrinks when `ηλ < 2`, holds its size at `ηλ = 2`, and grows past it. | [note](supporting/training/edge-of-stability.md) | [`EdgeOfStability`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/EdgeOfStability.lean) |
| Flat minima | Rescaling the parameters by `c` keeps every output, makes the loss flatter by `c²` and the fit farther by `\|c\|`. | [note](supporting/training/flat-minima.md) | [`Reparam`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/Reparam.lean) |
| Many small steps | A square-law loss over `N` steps is at most `c·D² / N`. A loss in proportion to the step gains nothing. | [note](supporting/training/small-steps.md) | [`SmallSteps`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/SmallSteps.lean) |
| Weight decay and early stopping | Weight decay `μ` leaves the share `μ / (λ + μ)` of the error. Stopping after `t` steps cuts at least as hard as `μ = 1 / (ηt)`. Spare features act the same way. | [note](supporting/training/weight-decay.md) | [`WeightDecay`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/WeightDecay.lean) |
| Batch size and learning rate | Two steps on two batches equal one step on both, minus `η²` times a term through the kernel between the batches. With averaged gradients that is the linear scaling rule. | [note](supporting/training/batch-size.md) | [`BatchSize`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/BatchSize.lean) |
| Chinchilla scaling | If the loss above its floor is `A / N + B / D` and compute fixes `N × D`, the best split has the two terms equal, so parameters and data grow together in the ratio `A / B`. The form itself is a measurement. | [note](supporting/training/compute-budget.md) | [`ComputeBudget`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/ComputeBudget.lean) |
| Softmax and cross-entropy | The loss pushes each score by prediction minus label, the same form as squared error. So a classifier moves through the same kernel, travels least, and has no hill between two settings. For a yes-or-no label no finite score is best. | [note](supporting/training/cross-entropy.md) | [`CrossEntropy`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/CrossEntropy.lean) |
| Dropout | With independent masks of variance `v`, the expected squared error is the plain squared error plus `v` times the squared shares "feature times weight". Over the data that is weight decay with a rate per feature, and plain weight decay when the features are equally large. | [note](supporting/training/dropout.md) | [`Dropout`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/Dropout.lean) |
| Perceptron, XOR, no free lunch | The margin is the distance from the data to the boundary, and the perceptron makes at most `(R / γ)²` mistakes. No flat boundary splits XOR; one lifted feature or one fold does. With no pattern in the labels, any guess at an unseen input is right on half of all labellings, and the flat line is best. | [note](supporting/training/perceptron.md) | [`Perceptron`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/Perceptron.lean) |
| Normalisation, deep linear networks, relabelling units | If rescaling the weights leaves the loss alone, the gradient is at right angles to them and every step makes them longer. For two weights multiplied, a step changes `a² − b²` only at second order, so the layers share the slope. Shuffling hidden units gives the same function. | [note](supporting/training/symmetry.md) | [`Symmetry`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/Symmetry.lean) |
| Adam, clipping, low-rank training, quantisation | Adam with no memory steps by the sign of the gradient, so a gap closes by `η` per step whatever the strength of the direction. Clipping caps travel at `t·η·c`. Confining the weights to a subspace never shortens the trip to a fit. Rounding the weights by `δ` moves an output by at most `‖δ‖` times the size of the features. | [note](supporting/training/step-rules.md) | [`StepRules`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/StepRules.lean) |

**What a fit does to new inputs.** Proofs in
[`lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs), notes in
[`docs/supporting/new-inputs/`](supporting/new-inputs).

| Topic | What is shown | Note | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Same kernel, same predictions | Two models with the same kernel give the same answer at a test point after every step, whatever their weights. | [note](supporting/new-inputs/same-kernel.md) | [`SameKernel`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/SameKernel.lean) |
| Benign overfitting | The nearest fit swallows the noise. With junk features of strength `c'` the damage at a new input is capped by `1 / c'`. | [note](supporting/new-inputs/benign-overfitting.md) | [`BenignOverfitting`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/BenignOverfitting.lean) |
| Bias and variance | With noise that is zero on average, the expected squared error at a new input is bias² plus variance. A floor `μ` on every direction raises the first and lowers the second. | [note](supporting/new-inputs/bias-variance.md) | [`BiasVariance`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/BiasVariance.lean) |
| Catastrophic forgetting | A step on a new task changes the old errors through the cross kernel only, and by at most the new task's travel. | [note](supporting/new-inputs/forgetting.md) | [`Forgetting`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/Forgetting.lean) |
| Lottery tickets | Pruning subtracts a kernel: slower and farther. It costs nothing where the dropped weights had no strength. | [note](supporting/new-inputs/lottery-ticket.md) | [`Pruning`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/Pruning.lean) |
| Dimpled manifold | A function can keep its loss on real data and still be one small step from any label, straight off the data manifold. | [note](supporting/new-inputs/dimpled-manifold.md) | [`Dimple`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/Dimple.lean) |
| Ensembles, weight averaging, diffusion | The squared error of an average is the average error minus the spread of its members, and the average is no steeper than the steepest member. For a model linear in its weights, averaging weights is averaging lines. The ideal denoiser is an attention head over the training examples, so it never leaves the region they span. | [note](supporting/new-inputs/averaging.md) | [`Averaging`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/Averaging.lean) |

**The shape of a network.** Proofs in
[`lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape), notes in
[`docs/supporting/network-shape/`](supporting/network-shape).

| Topic | What is shown | Note | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Depth separation | A network with one hidden layer needs `2^L` units to stay within one half of `L + 1` tent layers. | [note](supporting/network-shape/depth-separation.md) | [`DepthSeparation`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/DepthSeparation.lean) |
| Universal approximation | A one-hidden-layer ReLU network with `2n` units is within `2K / n` of any function on `[0, 1]` with slope at most `K`. The cost in units goes as slope over error. | [note](supporting/network-shape/universal-approximation.md) | [`UniversalApproximation`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/UniversalApproximation.lean) |
| Storage capacity | A model linear in `m` weights fits arbitrary labels on at most `m` points. A network with `b` bits of weights memorises every labelling of at most `b` inputs. Depth buys pattern, not storage. | [note](supporting/network-shape/capacity.md) | [`Capacity`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Capacity.lean) |
| Skip connections and normalisation | As optics: a skip is a bypass that never dims or blocks; a normalisation is an amplifier that restores full strength. | [note](supporting/network-shape/skip-and-norm.md) | [`SkipAndNorm`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/SkipAndNorm.lean) |
| Vanishing and exploding gradients | A per-layer gain `g` gives `g^L`: it dies below one and blows up above. The gradient comes back through the same plates, so it is dimmed the same way. | [note](supporting/network-shape/vanishing-gradients.md) | [`VanishingGradients`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/VanishingGradients.lean) |
| Information bottleneck | After any layer of a network that is right on every input: labels ≤ places occupied ≤ inputs. | [note](supporting/network-shape/information-bottleneck.md) | [`Bottleneck`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Bottleneck.lean) |
| Compression | A fold throws away one bit, so `L` folds merge at most `2^L` inputs. The average log-loss of any model, and the average length of any code, is at least the entropy. With description length as squared travel, gradient descent finds the fit that is cheapest to describe. | [note](supporting/network-shape/code-length.md) | [`CodeLength`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/CodeLength.lean) |
| Neural collapse | `K` unit vectors cannot all be farther apart than a simplex, and the simplex is the centred arrangement. | [note](supporting/network-shape/neural-collapse.md) | [`NeuralCollapse`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/NeuralCollapse.lean) |
| Mode connectivity | Linear in the weights: no hill between two fits. Two weights multiplied: the hill is exactly `t(1 − t)(a₁ − a₂)(b₁ − b₂)`. | [note](supporting/network-shape/mode-connectivity.md) | [`ModeConnectivity`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/ModeConnectivity.lean) |
| Attention and in-context learning | An attention head is a weighted average, so any stack of them keeps every token inside the region the tokens started in. One head without the softmax, reading examples as tokens, gives the prediction of one step of gradient descent on those examples. | [note](supporting/network-shape/attention.md) | [`Attention`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Attention.lean) |
| Signed distance functions | The theorem's distance is the distance field of the line, read at the data. The field never changes faster than one, however steep the line. A step shorter than the field cannot reach the line. The height above the line overstates the signed distance by at most `√(1 + K²)`. | [note](supporting/network-shape/signed-distance.md) | [`SignedDistance`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/SignedDistance.lean) |
| Slope of a network, adversarial examples, temperature | A network is no steeper than the product of its layers, and the gradient comes back through the same product. Flipping a classifier of slope `K` takes a move of at least `score / K`. Temperature changes the slope of the scores and never their order. A critic of slope one reports at most the distance. | [note](supporting/network-shape/network-slope.md) | [`NetworkSlope`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/NetworkSlope.lean) |
| He initialisation, the Bellman update | A fold keeps exactly half the mean squared size of a signal that is as often negative as positive, which is the factor two in He initialisation. One sweep of value updates has slope at most the discount `γ`, so `n` sweeps bring any two guesses within `γ^n`. | [note](supporting/network-shape/gain.md) | [`Gain`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Gain.lean) |
| The eikonal equation | Walking straight to a nearest point of the line, the distance field falls by exactly the distance walked. So the slope limit of one cannot be improved, whatever the line. | [note](supporting/network-shape/signed-distance.md) | [`Eikonal`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Eikonal.lean) |
| The GAN game | At one input the critic's loss is a cross-entropy whose label is the share of real data there. Where real and made data overlap, the best critic predicts that share; where they do not, no score is best and the critic is asked to be steeper forever. For the payoff `x * y`, each simultaneous step multiplies the squared distance from the resting point by `1 + η²`. | [note](supporting/training/gan-game.md) | [`GanGame`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/GanGame.lean) |
| Policy gradient | The log of a softmax policy's share is minus a cross-entropy against the label "this action was taken". So its push on the scores is "action taken minus prediction", and the expected reward changes at the rate: share of each action, times its reward, times that push. | [note](supporting/training/cross-entropy.md) | [`PolicyGradient`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/PolicyGradient.lean) |
| The median; random directions | Absolute error is least at a value with at most half the points on each side. If the coordinates of a random vector are uncorrelated with mean square `s`, its overlap with a fixed vector has mean square `s` times that vector's squared length: `1 / d` for unit vectors in `d` dimensions. | [note](supporting/new-inputs/mixing.md) | [`OnAverage`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/OnAverage.lean) |
| Nearest neighbours, k-means, contrastive learning | Copying the label of the nearest training input is wrong by at most the slope of the truth times the distance to it. Each half of a k-means step lowers the cost or leaves it. For vectors of length one, a softmax over cosine similarity at temperature `τ` is a softmax over squared distance, the weights of the ideal denoiser. | [note](supporting/new-inputs/neighbours.md) | [`Neighbours`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/Neighbours.lean) |
| Graph networks, the noising schedule, augmentation, guidance | A message-passing layer with every weight at least `m` shrinks the spread of the nodes by `1 − n m`. The diffusion noising schedule keeps signal plus noise at one while the signal's share falls as `(1 − β)^n`. A model averaged over a group of moves that keep distances ignores the moves and is no steeper. A baseline does not change the average push on a softmax policy. Classifier-free guidance is not an average: it leaves the stretch between its two predictions. | [note](supporting/new-inputs/mixing.md) | [`Mixing`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/Mixing.lean) |
| Counting | A read-out that moves smoothly from `k` to `k + 1` says `k + 1/2` on the way. The ideal denoiser, equally far from two examples, returns half of each. Telling `k` from `k + 1` takes slope at least one over the distance. | [note](supporting/new-inputs/counting.md) | [`Counting`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/Counting.lean) |
| AdamW | Weight decay put inside Adam's rescaling moves every weight by the same amount `η`, whatever the strength of the decay, and making the loss larger makes the decay weaker. Applied outside the rescaling it shrinks every weight by `1 − η μ` a step, as plain weight decay does. | [note](supporting/training/step-rules.md) | [`AdamW`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/AdamW.lean) |
| Schedules, momentum, SGD | With a changing step size the error left in a direction is the product of the factors `1 − η_t λ`. The step `2 / (m + M)` shrinks every direction by at least `(M − m) / (M + m)`. With momentum `β` the oscillation that never dies sits at `η λ = 2 (1 + β)`. An average of steps on parts of the data is one step on the averaged gradient. | [note](supporting/training/spectral-bias.md) | [`Optimisers`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/Optimisers.lean) |
| Lasso, input noise, adversarial training, mixup, distillation | Lasso sets a direction weaker than the penalty to exactly zero; weight decay only shrinks it. Noise of variance `σ²` on the input adds `σ² w²` to the loss of a linear model. The worst nudge of size `ε` lowers a linear score by exactly `ε` times the summed sizes of the weights. A model that fits every blend of two examples is straight between them. A teacher's soft label gives the student a finite best score. | [note](supporting/training/weight-decay.md) | [`Regularisers`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/Regularisers.lean) |
| RMSNorm and pre-norm | Dividing by the root mean square forgets the scale, so a loss read through it is scale invariant and its gradient is at right angles to the weights. A pre-norm branch adds the same whatever the size of the stream; a post-norm block always outputs root mean square one. | [note](supporting/training/symmetry.md) | [`RmsNorm`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/RmsNorm.lean) |
| Residual layers and the LSTM cell | Input plus a branch of slope `K` has slope between `1 − K` and `1 + K`. With `K < 1` it merges no two inputs and, with one input, never turns. `L` layers with branches of slope `c / L` have total slope at most `e^c`. The memory cell of an LSTM has the forget gate as its slope per step, and exactly one with the gate open. | [note](supporting/network-shape/skip-and-norm.md) | [`Residual`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Residual.lean) |
| Activations and caps on slope | A sigmoid's slope is never above one quarter, so `L` sigmoid layers have slope at most `(1/4)^L`. A leaky ReLU keeps a slope of at least its leak. The higher of two lines is no steeper than the steeper one. A layer divided by its own size has slope at most one. | [note](supporting/network-shape/vanishing-gradients.md) | [`Activations`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Activations.lean) |
| Convolution, stride, upsampling | A convolution layer is no steeper than the summed sizes of its kernel weights, whatever the length of the signal. After `L` layers of kernel width `n` an output depends only on the inputs within `L n` of it. Reading every second position is squeezing by two, so it doubles the slope; upsampling by two undoes it. | [note](supporting/network-shape/convolution.md) | [`Convolution`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Convolution.lean) |
| Mixture of experts, the VAE penalty, the softmax bottleneck | A gate that blends experts changes the output by at most what the experts change, plus their spread times the change in the gates. The penalty on a VAE code is at least half the squared distance of its centre from the origin, and zero only for the code that ignores its input. A table of scores made from `d` hidden numbers has rank at most `d`. | [note](supporting/network-shape/capacity.md) | [`Mixtures`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Mixtures.lean) |
| The straight-through trick, a cold softmax, Hopfield recall | A hard step has slope zero where it is not jumping; the trick `(hard - soft).detach() + soft` has the hard step's value and the stand-in's slope. At temperature `τ` the top score's share of a softmax is within `(n − 1) exp(−gap / τ)` of one. The update of a modern Hopfield network, for patterns and a state of length one, is the ideal denoiser. | [note](supporting/network-shape/attention.md) | [`HardChoices`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/HardChoices.lean) |
| Temperature, and the codebook snap | The average score of a sample from a softmax never falls as the temperature falls. Snapping a code to its nearest codebook entry moves the decoder's output by at most its slope times the distance to that entry. | [note](supporting/network-shape/network-slope.md) | [`Temperature`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Temperature.lean) |
| Attention loses rank with depth | With scores at most `B` in size, every token gets a share of at least `exp(−2B) / n`, so one layer of attention alone shrinks the spread of the tokens by `1 − exp(−2B)` and `L` layers by that to the power `L`. | [note](supporting/network-shape/attention.md) | [`AttentionRate`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/AttentionRate.lean) |
| Kolmogorov–Arnold networks | A node that adds one learnable curve per input is within `2 × total slope / n` of a ReLU layer with `n` pieces per edge. | [note](supporting/network-shape/universal-approximation.md) | [`KolmogorovArnold`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/KolmogorovArnold.lean) |

## Paper by paper

Well-known papers and ideas, each said in the terms of the squiggly line,
with the file that proves it. Every statement is for a simplified model,
most often a fixed kernel. They are grouped by which of six things they
connect through: slope, nearness, travel, the kernel and the lift, folds,
and averages. What is not proved yet is in
[`wishlist.md`](wishlist.md). The papers and years are recalled
from memory and have not been checked.

### Slope

| Paper or idea | In squiggly terms | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| Robustness against accuracy (Tsipras et al. 2019) | `vertical / √(1 + K²) ≤ nearest ≤ vertical`: a line can be near the data only by being steep. | [`Sandwich.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Theorem/Sandwich.lean) |
| Slope of a network (Szegedy et al. 2014; Bartlett et al. 2017; Miyato et al. 2018) | A network is no steeper than the product of the slopes of its layers. So its nearness to the data has to be paid for in that product. | [`NetworkSlope.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/NetworkSlope.lean) |
| Backpropagation (Rumelhart, Hinton and Williams 1986) | The gradient comes back through the same layers in reverse, so it is bounded by the same product. | [`NetworkSlope.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/NetworkSlope.lean), [`VanishingGradients.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/VanishingGradients.lean) |
| Vanishing gradients and the LSTM (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber 1997) | A slope below one per layer dies out with depth, above one blows up. | [`VanishingGradients.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/VanishingGradients.lean) |
| Adversarial examples (Szegedy et al. 2014; Goodfellow et al. 2015) | To flip a classifier of slope `K` you must move the input at least `score / K`. For a linear model, nudging every input by `ε` moves the score by `ε` times the sum of the sizes of the weights. | [`NetworkSlope.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/NetworkSlope.lean) |
| Dimpled manifold (Shamir et al. 2021) | Gentle along the data, steep straight off it. | [`Dimple.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/Dimple.lean) |
| Generative adversarial networks, the original game (Goodfellow et al. 2014) | The critic's loss at an input is a cross-entropy whose label is the real share there. Where real and made data overlap the best critic is finite. Where they do not, no score is best, so the critic is asked to be steeper forever. Simultaneous steps of the two players spiral away from the resting point. | one input at a time, and for the payoff `x * y`: [`GanGame.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/GanGame.lean) |
| Wasserstein GAN (Arjovsky et al. 2017) | The critic is held to slope one. The gap it reports between two points is then at most their distance, and a distance field is such a critic. | [`NetworkSlope.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/NetworkSlope.lean) |
| Temperature and calibration (Guo et al. 2017; Holtzman et al. 2020) | Dividing the scores by a temperature divides their slope and never changes their order. | [`NetworkSlope.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/NetworkSlope.lean) |
| Softmax and cross-entropy | For a yes-or-no label no finite score is best, so the loss keeps asking for a steeper line. | [`CrossEntropy.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/CrossEntropy.lean) |
| Label smoothing (Szegedy et al. 2016) | With a soft label the best score is finite, so the slope can stop growing. | [`CrossEntropy.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/CrossEntropy.lean) |
| Reinforcement learning (Bellman 1957; Mnih et al. 2015) | One sweep of value updates is a map of slope `γ < 1`, so repeated sweeps pull every guess to one answer. | [`Gain.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Gain.lean) |
| Signed distance networks (Park et al. 2019) | The distance field of any line has slope at most one. | [`SignedDistance.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/SignedDistance.lean) |
| Residual networks keep points apart (He et al. 2016) | Input plus a branch of slope `K < 1` keeps any two points at least `1 − K` of their distance apart. A skip cannot merge what a fold would. | [`Residual.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Residual.lean) |
| Residual networks and turns (He et al. 2016) | With one input, input plus a branch of slope below one is always rising. So a residual layer adds a turn only where its branch is steeper than one. Squiggle has to be bought in the branch. | [`Residual.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Residual.lean) |
| Residual networks at depth (He et al. 2016) | `L` layers with branches of slope `c / L` have total slope at most `e^c`, however deep. A plain stack of slope `K` has `K^L`. | [`Residual.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Residual.lean) |
| LSTM and GRU gates (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber 1997; Cho et al. 2014) | Along the memory cell the slope per step is the forget gate, which is at most one. With the gate open it is exactly one: a skip through time. | [`Residual.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Residual.lean), for the memory cell |
| Sigmoid and tanh saturate (Glorot and Bengio 2010; Nair and Hinton 2010) | A sigmoid's slope is never above one quarter. `L` sigmoid layers shrink the gradient by `4^L` unless the weights make up for it. A fold's slope is one where it is open. | [`Activations.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Activations.lean), for the sigmoid |
| Leaky ReLU (Maas et al. 2013) | A leaky fold keeps a slope of at least the leak, so it never merges two inputs and never dies. | [`Activations.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Activations.lean) |
| Max pooling | The highest of several lines is no steeper than the steepest of them. | [`Activations.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Activations.lean), for two lines |
| Spectral normalisation (Miyato et al. 2018) | A layer divided by its own size has slope at most one, so a stack of them does too. The cap on slope is put in by hand. | [`Activations.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Activations.lean), for one layer |
| Adversarial training (Madry et al. 2018) | For a linear model, training against the worst nudge of size `ε` is training with a penalty of `ε` times the summed sizes of the weights. The defence is a charge on slope. | [`Regularisers.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/Regularisers.lean), for a linear model |
| Training with noise on the input (Bishop 1995) | For a linear model, noise of variance `σ²` on the input adds `σ²` times the squared slope to the expected loss. Noise is a charge on slope. | [`Regularisers.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/Regularisers.lean), for a linear model |
| Mixup (Zhang et al. 2018) | A model that fits every blend of two examples is straight between them. Its slope there is the gap in labels over the distance, the least any line through both can have. | [`Regularisers.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/Regularisers.lean) |
| Knowledge distillation (Hinton et al. 2015) | A teacher's soft label gives the student a finite best score, the teacher's own. A hard label gives none. So a student is never asked for more slope than its teacher has. | [`Regularisers.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/Regularisers.lean) |
| Convolution, slope (LeCun et al. 1989) | A convolution layer is no steeper than the summed sizes of its kernel weights, whatever the size of the image. | [`Convolution.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Convolution.lean) |
| Convolution, locality (LeCun et al. 1989) | After `L` layers of kernel radius `r`, an output depends only on inputs within `L × r` of it. Squiggles are made locally and shared across positions. | [`Convolution.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Convolution.lean) |
| Stride and downsampling | Reading every second input is squeezing by two, in the sense of [`Compression.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Theorem/Compression.lean). Each stride doubles the frequency of what the later layers see. | [`Convolution.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Convolution.lean) |
| Upsampling (Ronneberger et al. 2015) | The mirror of stride: stretching by two halves the frequency. | [`Convolution.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Convolution.lean) |
| Mixture of experts and gating (Jacobs et al. 1991) | A gate that blends experts adds slope only through how far apart the experts are. Where the experts agree, a gate adds none. | [`Mixtures.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Mixtures.lean) |
| Straight-through estimator (Bengio et al. 2013) | A hard step such as rounding has slope zero almost everywhere, so no gradient comes back through it. The trick keeps the value of the hard step and borrows the slope of a gentle stand-in. | [`HardChoices.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/HardChoices.lean) |
| Gumbel-softmax (Jang et al. 2017; Maddison et al. 2017) | A softmax at temperature `τ` is a gentle stand-in for picking the top score. As `τ` falls it tends to the pick itself, and its slope grows without limit. | [`HardChoices.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/HardChoices.lean), for the cold softmax. The Gumbel noise is not shown |
| Temperature trades quality for diversity (Ackley et al. 1985; Holtzman et al. 2020) | As the temperature falls, the average score of a sample rises and the samples concentrate on the top score. Temperature is the slope of the scores. | [`Temperature.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Temperature.lean), [`HardChoices.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/HardChoices.lean) |
| Why image models miscount | A count is a step. A smooth read-out says `k + 1/2` between `k` and `k + 1`, the ideal denoiser blends two examples, and telling counts apart costs slope. This shows the difficulty, not the cause of real errors. | [`Counting.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/Counting.lean) |

| XOR (Minsky and Papert 1969) | The smallest data that no flat boundary fits. One lift, or one fold, fixes it. | [`Perceptron.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/Training/Perceptron.lean) |
| XOR by changing the encoding (one input per corner, or one class per corner) | A table lookup is a lift that fits anything. | Sphere tracing (Hart 1996) | A step shorter than the distance field cannot reach the line. | [`SignedDistance.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/SignedDistance.lean) |
| The eikonal equation (classical) | Walking straight to the nearest point of the line, the distance falls by exactly what is walked. | Nearest neighbours (Cover and Hart 1967) | Copying the label of the nearest training input is wrong by at most the slope of the truth times the distance to that input. | [`Neighbours.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/Neighbours.lean) |
| k-means and vector quantisation (Lloyd 1982; van den Oord et al. 2017) | Each half of a step brings the data nearer to its codes: moving a point to its nearest code, and moving a code to the average of its points. The cost never rises. | [`Neighbours.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/Neighbours.lean) |
| Contrastive learning (Chen et al. 2020; Radford et al. 2021) | For vectors of length one, a softmax over cosine similarity is a softmax over squared distance. | Codebook autoencoders, VQ-VAE (van den Oord et al. 2017) | Snapping a code to its nearest codebook entry moves the decoder's output by at most its slope times that distance. The codebook update is a k-means step, and the gradient passes the snap by the straight-through trick. | [`Temperature.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/Temperature.lean), [`Neighbours.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NewInputs/Neighbours.lean), [`HardChoices.lean`](../lean/SquigglyLine/Supporting/NetworkShape/HardChoices.lean) |
